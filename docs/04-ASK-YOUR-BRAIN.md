# 04 — ASK YOUR BRAIN

Once you have added real information, you can start asking useful questions.

This is what `CLAUDE.md` treats as **Ask a question** — you can just say it in your own words, like "Ask my brain..."

## Example question

Type this in Claude:

> Ask my brain: Would Sarah potentially be interested in this property, and what information are we still missing?

This works best right after you have already added:

- Sarah's investor criteria
- a property note for a possible deal

## What a good answer should do

A good answer should:

- connect Sarah's stored preferences to the property facts already in your brain
- explain **why** the property may or may not fit
- clearly separate **what is known** from **what is still unknown**
- point out missing facts that matter before making a stronger recommendation
- avoid inventing facts that were never added

For example, a grounded answer might say:

- Sarah wants Tampa properties under $400,000
- she prefers light renovations
- she wants at least a 10% cash-on-cash return
- this property may fit some of that, but key numbers may still be missing
- we may still need repair budget, taxes, insurance, rent confidence, or financing assumptions

## What if the answer feels uncertain?

That is normal.

Your brain can only work from what has actually been added so far. If important facts are missing, Claude should say that instead of pretending to know.

## One important expectation

Answers are **not perfectly scripted**. Two good answers may not use the exact same wording every time.

What matters is that the answer is grounded, useful, and honest about what is known versus missing.
