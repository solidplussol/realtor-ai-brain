# 03 — ADD KNOWLEDGE

Now that your brain exists, the next step is simple: add one real piece of information.

This is what `CLAUDE.md` calls **Add new material** — you can just say it in your own words, like "Add this to my brain."

## Your first example

Type this in Claude:

> Add this to my brain: Sarah is an investor looking for properties in Tampa under $400,000. She prefers light renovations and wants at least a 10% cash-on-cash return.

## What should happen

Conceptually, Claude should understand that:

- **Sarah** is a person your business may work with
- she is an **investor**
- she is looking in **Tampa**
- her budget is **under $400,000**
- she prefers **light renovations**
- she wants at least a **10% cash-on-cash return**

Claude should then create or update the right note or notes in your brain so this information is remembered in an organized way.

Claude should also keep the original raw material, such as items in the Inbox, and keep track of where the information came from so nothing is lost.

You do **not** need to tell Claude which folder or note to use. The point is to speak naturally.

## A second simple example

You can also add a property note in the same style.

For example:

> Add this to my brain: 412 Oak Terrace is a 3-bedroom property in Tampa listed at $365,000. It needs cosmetic updates, the roof was replaced in 2021, and the current rent estimate is about $2,700 per month.

## What should happen with the property example

Claude should treat that as a property-related fact set and organize it into the brain in a way that can be used later.

It may create a new property note, update an existing one, or connect it to other notes that already exist.

## What to look for afterward

After you add something:

- look in Claude's response for a clear summary of what it updated
- look in Obsidian for new or changed notes
- look in GitHub Desktop's **Changes** tab (the list of files that changed) if you want to see that files changed

You are building the habit of speaking naturally and letting the brain stay organized behind the scenes.
